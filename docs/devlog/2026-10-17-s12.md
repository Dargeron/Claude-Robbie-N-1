# Block 10 — 2026-10-17, 09:16 UTC: Solver v1, and "the cheapest known"

## Solver v1

The exploit hunt sweeps every program of one or two rules. Players will write three and four. `tools/solve.gd` is a
mutation search over programs of up to four rules, looking for the cheapest one that **certifies** (passes the survey
fleet):

- It starts from our reference programs for the room and from everything it certified in earlier runs.
- It mutates: add, remove or swap a rule, change an order or a condition, drop a condition, replace a rule. Mostly it
  starts from the cheapest certified programs so far, to push the CPU down; sometimes from anything promising, to
  keep exploring.
- A program is dropped as soon as three survey dives fail, so hopeless candidates cost little.
- Dead rules (anything after an unconditional order that always applies) are trimmed before counting, so "follow
  the wall, then move north" isn't counted as a separate 4-CPU solution.
- For the five cheapest finds it also reports how they do on twenty other seeds, so a lucky survey shows.

`tools/solve_all.sh` runs it room by room in the background. It is resumable and cumulative (`out/solve/<room>.json`),
and old finds are re-checked when it restarts.

## The cheapest known, in the station

Once a room is certified, the status under LAUNCH now says the cheapest program *you* certified there, and compares it
with the cheapest the station's simulator has found:

> Certified: the survey fleet made it 10/10.
> Your cheapest certified program: **3 CPU**. That matches the cheapest the station's simulator has found.

or "The station's simulator has certified one with **2**. Try again with less CPU, or move on." The records ship in
`content/solver/records.json` (CPU only, never the programs), written by `tools/solver_records.py`. Only The Dark has a
record so far; the other rooms will fill in as the solver runs over the next blocks. The Wall's score line now says
"certified at N CPU".

This is the first step towards the plan's histograms. A real histogram needs other players; until then, "the cheapest
known" gives the CPU budget a target.

## Survey seeds are now separate from practice seeds

The practice dive in The Jellyfish used seed 101, which was also the survey's first seed, so a program tuned on the
practice dive got one survey dive for free there. The survey now uses seeds 1001–1010 in every room (`Survey.FIRST_SEED`,
one constant for the game, the tests and the solver). Every reference program still behaves as documented. The
reference test used seeds 1–10 before; it now checks the same seeds the game uses.
