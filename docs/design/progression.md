## In one paragraph

Reaching the exit opens the next room, so nobody gets stuck. Every room also has a few optional goals, and each pays
once. **Salvage** comes from things Robbie brings back: caches lying in the rooms and the wrecks of earlier Robbies.
It buys **equipment** in the Lab. **Data** comes from what Robbie learns: mapping, first sightings, surveys and lean
programs. It buys **instructions** and **CPU**. Robbie has a few slots for equipment, so each dive is a choice of what
to carry.

## 1. Moving on

- **Reaching the exit once opens the next room.** The survey is no longer required.
- **The survey fleet becomes an optional goal** (16 of 20 through), which pays Data. "Certified" stays as a badge on
  the room's tab and on the Wall.

## 2. The two currencies

| | Salvage | Data |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | Parts: metal, glass, wire | Knowledge: maps, sightings, results |
| Earned by | Touching caches in rooms; reaching the wreck of an earlier Robbie | Charting a room; first sightings; surveys; lean programs; first time lost to something new |
| Spent on | Equipment and slots (the Lab) | Instructions and CPU |
| Feels like | Going somewhere dangerous to grab something | Getting better at the game |

Why two: they reward different play. Salvage pulls Robbie off the safe path, into risk. Data rewards understanding,
including from losses. Neither can be farmed: every source pays once.

**Wrecks** (recommended): a lost Robbie leaves its wreck where it fell. The next Robbie that touches it recovers
parts. Every loss becomes a little useful. The Wall of Robbies gets a meaning ("Robbie 012's wreck: recovered by
Robbie 015"), and so does the slow story ("the wrecks are not where we left them...").

With today's six rooms, a player who does everything earns about 400 Data and 150 Salvage. That's enough for most of
the first shop, so the shop needs a second region of rooms to grow into.

## 4. What you can buy

**Instructions (Data).** The order or condition a room teaches is given free when the room opens: Mission Control
"uploads" it. Alternatives and refinements are bought. That keeps every room solvable with what you have, while
buying gives you new ways to solve it.

- Free with the rooms: move towards, follow wall, stop, lamp, keep distance, shoot, hostile near, valve shut.
- To buy (examples): wall behind, energy below, executing, move towards light, open water, valve open, `move` by
  heading. Some of these are free today; a save that already uses them keeps them.

**CPU (Data).** A CPU module adds +2 CPU to every room's budget, in three levels (cost 40, 80, 120). The rooms stay
tuned as they are; the module is how a clumsy program fits (note #8).

**Equipment (Salvage), carried in slots.** Robbie starts with 3 slots: lamp, laser and one free. More slots cost
Salvage (2 more at most, so choosing stays part of the game). Ideas for parts, each changing a room's puzzle rather
than just making numbers bigger:

- **Dim lamp:** a lamp setting that lights only 3 tiles. Jellyfish care less, and you see less.
- **Spare battery:** +25% energy, takes a slot.
- **Hull plate:** +1 knock, takes a slot.
- **Sonar ping:** shows camouflaged lurkers for a moment, every few seconds. Answers note #14 in a new way.
- **Soft fins:** quieter swimming, so eels hear Robbie at 2 tiles instead of 3.
- **Drag anchor:** stop in place even in a current (for later rooms with currents).

The laser itself could become something you take or leave: leaving it frees a slot.
