# The hangar (the Lab): ideas for Albert to read first

Status: **brainstorm, nothing built**, 2026-10-25. Albert asked for ideas on how the Lab fits the console world before
any of it is made. These lean darker, more political and more cynical, as he asked. The plot they serve: **Robbie's
consciousness and freedom, and the intelligence in Jupiter's depths, against the all-powerful Company.**

## The world in one paragraph (shared with the console)

The Company owns the station, the sonar, the cards, the type plates, the hulls, the air, and the operator's contract
(passage to Jupiter, paid back in shifts). It calls Robbies *units*, *hulls* or *assets*, and their last words
*telemetry artefacts*. Each Robbie is the same mind pressed into a new hull from a master pattern the Company keeps
under seal; it is not supposed to remember the one before. The deep is not empty: wrecks are not where they fell, and
something down there answers on frequencies the Company doesn't publish. From Jeff VanderMeer's *Borne* and *Dead
Astronauts* we take the Company as a corporation that makes living things and owns them, the ruined ecology it leaves
behind, beings it made that turn out to have minds of their own, and a story told in fragments and paperwork. From
*Annihilation*, the bureaucracy that studies something it cannot understand and lies to its own staff about it.

## What the hangar is

One room behind the console, reached through the hangar window. It is **the one warm room**: a work lamp, Bathy in
full colour hanging from a chain hoist, the smell of oil you can almost see. Everything else in it belongs to the
Company, and you feel it: the requisition hatch, the inventory stencils, the camera in the corner, the audit tags on
every part.

What you do there:

1. **Fit parts.** Parts hang on a pegboard. Drag one onto a socket on Bathy's hull and he changes: the sprite, the
   lamp's shape on the scope, the drums on the keypunch (a new part brings new orders or conditions), the card
   reader's capacity (CPU), his energy. He reacts in his face screen: he likes the new lamp, he flinches at the laser.
2. **Requisition.** Parts and type plates are not bought in a shop; they are **requested** from the Company on a form
   (Papers, Please). Fill in the part code, tick a justification, sign, stamp, send it up the tube. The answer comes
   at the start of the next shift: APPROVED, DENIED (with a reason that is not a reason), or APPROVED IN PART. What the
   Company grants depends on your record: certified sectors, hulls lost, compliance.
3. **Build hulls.** The rack of spare hulls is finite. Each hull is built from Salvage. Run out and the shift ends
   early, with a notice.
4. **Salvage.** Lost Robbies can be recovered from where they fell, if a later Robbie reaches the wreck (a card:
   *recover wreck*). Salvage comes back with marks on it: scratches in patterns, growths, a part that was not fitted
   when he left.

## The mechanic that carries the plot: the Governor

Every hull leaves the hangar with a **Compliance Governor** fitted: a sealed Company module in the best socket.

- It **takes CPU** (a third of the card reader at first) and it forces a hidden card at the top of every deck:
  *transmit telemetry*, *avoid Company property*, later *return to dock when ordered*.
- You **cannot remove it** at first. Op. 5's notes explain how to open the seal. Doing it frees CPU and changes
  how Robbie talks (longer last words, questions, a name for himself).
- The Company **audits**: a random hull is inspected each shift. Get caught and the next memo is a Notice; get caught
  three times and the story turns (a new operator is "assigned to assist you").
- The choice is never forced. A player who keeps the Governor plays a fine puzzle game with a tighter budget and an
  unsettling story. A player who removes it plays an easier game with a darker one.

This is the Papers, Please engine (efficiency against conscience) put in Robbie's hull.

## A second plot mechanic: what Robbie N+1 remembers

The pattern is supposed to be wiped clean for each hull. In the hangar sits the **pattern press**, where a new hull is
imprinted. With a part from Op. 5's locker you can let a little through: the previous Robbie's route as a ghost on
the scope (today's ghost, now a story beat), a hunch (a free card the previous Robbie had), his fear of a creature.
The Company calls it *pattern contamination*. Robbie calls it *remembering*.

## The deep's side

Later, salvage brings parts nobody made: a lamp that glows a colour the sonar can't show, a sensor that hears the
eels' language. They work. They don't fit the Company's sockets, so they need Op. 5's adapters. Fitting them is how
the deep starts to talk to Robbie, and through him, to you. Whether it is a friend is left open.

## Three moods to choose from

- **A. The warm workshop.** As in the console proposal: oil, lamp, Bathy on the hoist; the Company only as paperwork.
  Comforting; the darkness stays in the memos.
- **B. The Company floor.** Cold: inventory stencils, a camera, the requisition hatch, numbered hulls under sheets
  like bodies. Bathy is the only warm thing. Bleak and very Papers, Please.
- **C. The vats.** VanderMeer's biotech: hulls are partly grown, the pattern press is a tank, spare hulls float in
  fluid. Strange and horrible; the strongest link to *Borne*, the furthest from steampunk.

**Recommended: B with a warm corner of A.** The hangar is a Company floor; your workbench is the one lit corner where
Bathy hangs. C appears later as the deep's parts and the pattern press, not as the room.
